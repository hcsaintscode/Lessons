Sending Bits

Everything is 1s and 0s

Why Binary?

Computers send everything as just two signals: on or off, 1 or 0.
Two states are easy to tell apart — even with a weak or noisy signal.

Every Device Needs an Address

An IP address is like a home address for a computer — it tells data where to go.
Routers are the post offices that pick the road.

Redundancy = Backup Paths

Cables get cut. Routers fail. The Internet keeps working because there's always more than one path.
Road closed? Take another route to the same place.

Packets

Big messages are broken into small numbered packets, sent separately, and reassembled at the end.
Lost a packet? Ask for just that one again.

Two Ways to Send

  • TCP — everything arrives, in order (web pages, email). A little slower.
  • UDP — fast, but might drop pieces (video calls, gaming).
Which would you use for a video call?

Design a Reliable Network

Design a network for a place YOU care about — home, classroom, your gaming setup. Then add backup paths so it survives any one failure.
