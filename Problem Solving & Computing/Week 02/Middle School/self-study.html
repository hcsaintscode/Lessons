Lesson 1

What Makes a Computer a Computer?

A computer is a machine that works with information. It takes in information, does something to it, and gives new information back out. That's really the whole job.

A toaster is a machine too — but it can only ever make toast. What makes a computer special is that it can be reprogrammed to do almost any job you can describe. The same laptop can be a calculator, a drawing pad, a music player, or a game — just by running different software.

  • A phone, a laptop, and a game console are all computers.
  • So are the hidden computers inside a car, a microwave, and a smartwatch.
  • They look different, but underneath they all do the same four things.

Those four things have a name: Input, Output, Storage, and Processing — IOSP for short. Everything a computer does is one of these four.

Lesson 2

IOSP — The Four Things Every Computer Does

No matter how fancy a computer is, everything it does fits into four buckets. Learn these four and you can understand any device — or design your own.

Input — information going in

  • How you tell the computer something.
  • Examples: tapping a screen, typing keys, a camera, a microphone, a GPS sensor.

Output — information coming out

  • How the computer tells you something back.
  • Examples: pictures on a screen, sounds from a speaker, a phone buzzing, a printed page.

Processing — the thinking

  • The work the computer does to turn input into output.
  • Examples: doing math, sorting a list, deciding what to show next, checking a password.

Storage — remembering for later

  • Where the computer keeps information so it isn't lost.
  • Examples: your saved photos, your game progress, your contacts, a high-score list.

Every app is just IOSP: Input → Processing (+ Storage) → Output. Something goes in, the computer does something with it (sometimes remembering it), and something comes back out. That's the pattern behind every device you've ever used.

Lesson 3

Four Kinds of Processing

"Processing" sounds mysterious, but most of it is just four simple moves happening very fast. Once you can spot these, you'll notice them everywhere.

  • If / Then — make a decision: if the password is correct, then unlock the phone.
  • Comparing — check which is bigger, smaller, or equal: is this score higher than the high score?
  • Finding a Match — search for something: find the contact named "Mom" in your list.
  • Counting — keep a tally: count how many likes a post has, or steps you took today.

When you design your own app later, "Processing" just means picking which of these moves your app makes to turn its input into its output.

Wrap-Up

The Big Idea

Every computer — and every app — is Input, Output, Storage, and Processing. Once you can see those four buckets, no device is a mystery anymore. And designing something new is just deciding what goes in each bucket.
